CRITICAL ISSUES (3)
Critical
User control and freedom
Forced account creation blocks ticket purchases
When a first-time visitor lands on bigticket.ae excited to buy a ticket, they quickly discover there's no way to purchase without first creating a full account. The registration flow requires a mobile number, email, a complex password (with uppercase, lowercase, number, special character), and phone verification — that's at least 3 screens and 2 minutes before they can even see a payment page. For an impulse-driven product like lottery tickets, this is a conversion killer. Every extra step between 'I want to buy' and 'I've bought' loses a percentage of customers, and forced registration is the single biggest friction point in e-commerce.
Add a guest checkout option that only requires an email address and phone number to complete a purchase. Collect the rest of the profile information after the purchase is confirmed, when the user is already invested. At minimum, offer 'Buy as Guest' alongside the sign-in prompt. This alone could increase first-time purchase conversion by 20-30% based on industry benchmarks for lottery and gaming products.

Critical
Help and documentation
No FAQ, help section, or responsible play information anywhere
A lottery platform dealing with real money transactions has zero visible help resources. There's no FAQ page answering basic questions like 'How do draws work?', 'What happens if I win?', 'How do I collect my prize?', or 'What are the odds?' There's no live chat, no help centre link, and critically, no responsible gambling information — no spend limits, no self-exclusion options, no links to problem gambling support. For first-time visitors from outside the UAE who may not understand how Big Ticket works, this lack of information creates uncertainty that directly blocks purchases.
Create a comprehensive FAQ/Help section accessible from the main navigation. Include draw mechanics, odds, prize collection process, payment methods, and account management. Add a dedicated 'Responsible Play' page with self-exclusion tools, spend limits, cooling-off periods, and links to gambling support organisations. This isn't just good UX — it's likely a regulatory requirement for licensed lottery operations in Abu Dhabi and builds the trust needed for users to hand over their payment details.

Critical
Flexibility and efficiency of use
No search, filtering, or quick-purchase options anywhere
The entire site has zero search functionality. Users can't search for past draw results, look up ticket numbers, find specific winner stories, or quickly navigate to a product they want. The Winner's Club page shows dozens of winner cards in an infinite scroll with no way to filter by anything meaningful — not by date, prize type, nationality, or amount. For returning customers who buy monthly, there's no 'Buy again' or saved preferences to speed up repeat purchases. Everything requires starting from scratch every single time.
Add a site-wide search bar in the navigation. On the Winner's Club page, add filter controls for draw series, prize amount range, nationality, and date range. For logged-in users, add a 'Quick Buy' feature that remembers their last ticket selection and payment method for one-tap repeat purchases. These efficiency features reward loyal customers and reduce the time from intent to purchase, which directly impacts monthly recurring revenue.

MAJOR ISSUES (5)
Major
Aesthetic and minimalist design
Homepage is visually cluttered with competing elements
The homepage tries to do everything at once — hero banner, countdown timer, winner highlights, promotional cards, ticket grid, sign-in overlay, statistics, and footer all fight for attention. There's no clear visual hierarchy telling users where to look first or what action to take. The sign-in overlay partially blocks the ticket grid, creating a frustrating experience where content is visible but not accessible. The red/gold/white colour palette feels more carnival than premium, undermining the 'dream big' brand positioning for prizes worth millions of AED.
Redesign the homepage with a clear visual hierarchy: one dominant hero with a single CTA ('Buy Tickets'), followed by trust signals (winners), then secondary actions. Remove the sign-in overlay or make it dismissible with a clear X button. Establish a more refined colour system — keep the red as an accent but use more white space and a cleaner layout to convey the premium nature of multi-million dirham prizes.

Major
Consistency and standards
Each page looks like a different website
The visual identity shifts dramatically between pages: the homepage uses a light background, Winner's Club has a red gradient header, Live Draw uses a golden/yellow header, and the auth pages use an orange dashed border. CTA buttons alternate between red fills, white outlines, and grey states with no consistent pattern. This inconsistency makes the site feel unprofessional and erodes user confidence — if the visual experience feels disjointed, users may question whether the entire operation is trustworthy.
Create and enforce a consistent design system: one header style, one colour palette, consistent CTA button hierarchy (primary = solid, secondary = outlined, tertiary = text). Replace the orange dashed border on auth forms with the same card style used elsewhere. Every page should feel unmistakably 'Big Ticket' within the first second of landing.

Major
Error prevention
No inline form validation or purchase confirmation step
None of the form fields on the sign-in or registration pages show real-time validation. Users fill out the entire form, hit submit, and only then discover their password doesn't meet requirements or their phone number is invalid. For a platform processing financial transactions, the absence of a clear confirmation step before purchase is concerning — users should see a summary of what they're buying, the total cost, and their payment details before committing.
Add inline validation that checks each field as the user types or moves to the next field — green checkmarks for valid entries, clear error messages for invalid ones. Before any purchase is finalised, show a confirmation screen summarising: ticket numbers, draw date, total price, and payment method with a clear 'Confirm Purchase' button. This reduces payment disputes, support tickets, and accidental purchases.

Major
Visibility of system status
Registration stepper doesn't clearly show progress
The Create Account page has a 3-step stepper (Account Details → Verify → Personal Info) but the inactive steps look too similar to the active step — just slightly greyed out numbers. Users can't quickly tell which step they've completed, which they're on, and how many remain. Combined with no back button visible, users feel uncertain about the process and may abandon registration if it feels like it's going to take too long.
Redesign the stepper with clear visual differentiation: completed steps get a green checkmark, the current step is highlighted in the brand colour with a filled circle, and future steps are distinctly dimmed. Add a 'Back' link on each step and show estimated time remaining (e.g., 'Step 2 of 3 — about 30 seconds left'). This reduces registration abandonment by setting clear expectations.

MINOR ISSUES (4)
Minor
Match between system and real world
'Series 285' terminology is confusing for new visitors
The countdown timer on every page references 'Series 285 | 3rd Apr 2026, 7:30 PM' — but nowhere on the site explains what a 'series' means. New visitors, especially international ones, have no context for this lottery-specific terminology. It's insider language that makes new users feel like outsiders rather than welcome participants.
Add a brief subtitle or tooltip: 'Series 285 = April 2026 Draw' or 'Draw #285 — 3rd April 2026'. Better yet, replace 'Series' with 'Draw' in the UI entirely, since that's the word regular people use when talking about lottery events. Keep 'Series' in the fine print for official/regulatory purposes.
